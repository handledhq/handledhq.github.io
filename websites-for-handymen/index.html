Before you build

What handymen need from a website (and what they don't).

Most guides list fifty features. A handyman site needs a handful, done well. Here's what turns a visitor into a call.

Every job, named

People search for the job, not the trade: "patch drywall", "fix garbage disposal", "hang a TV". Name each job you take on, in their words.

Towns, not a radius

List the towns you'll drive to. Customers know right away if you cover them, and Google gets a clear signal of where you work.

Real reviews, word for word

Your Google reviews and rating, exactly as customers wrote them. Never made-up testimonials.

One tap to reach you

Call and text buttons that stay in reach on a phone. Plenty of people find a handyman standing in front of the problem, phone in hand.

Photos of your own work

Before-and-after shots from real jobs beat stock photos. We start with your Google photos and add new ones whenever you send them.

Honest limits

If you're licensed or insured, say so. If a job needs a licensed plumber or electrician where you work, say you don't do it. Clear limits build trust.

Skip the extras

No blog you'll never update, no chat bot, no slideshow. A fast page with your jobs, towns, reviews and number does the work.

Fast on a phone

A page that takes seconds to load loses the call to the next result. We keep it light so it opens quickly on a phone.

Your own site vs. lead sites vs. doing it yourself

Lead sites (Thumbtack, Angi, HomeAdvisor) can send work, but you pay for leads, and the customer sees other handymen right next to you. Keep them if they pay. Your own site is the one place where it's only you, and it gives the people who already heard your name somewhere to land.

Website builders (Wix, Squarespace, GoDaddy) work if you have the evenings for it. If you'd rather spend them on jobs, send it to us: we build it from your Google listing and reviews, and you see it finished before you pay.
