Questions

Good questions.

Why do Apple Maps, Bing and Nextdoor matter if I'm on Google?

They're where a lot of customers look first. iPhones open addresses in Apple Maps, Windows computers and Bing search use Bing Places, and neighbors ask Nextdoor for recommendations. Each one keeps its own listing, separate from Google, and most local businesses have never claimed them.

How do I list my business on Apple Maps?

Through Apple Business Connect, Apple's free tool for business owners. We claim or create your place card there, verify it and fill it out. If that's all you need, it's $99 on its own.

My Bing Places verification is stuck. Can you fix it?

Usually. Bing offers a few ways to verify, and some businesses can import from their Google listing. If the postcard never came or the code expired, we try a different path where Bing offers one. Microsoft makes the final call, so we can't promise a date.

My Nextdoor business page is already claimed. What now?

That usually means an old employee, a past owner or a marketing company claimed it. We go through Nextdoor's process to get the page back to you. Nextdoor decides, so we can't promise how fast, but we keep pushing and keep you posted.

Do you need my Google listing to be right first?

It helps, because everything gets matched to it. If your Google listing is thin or out of date, we can fill that out too for $149, so every map shows the same thing.

What do you need from me?

Your business details, a few good photos, and access: either you add us as a user on each account, or you give us the logins the job needs, which you can change when we're done. Some maps verify by calling or texting the business number, so we'll tell you when to expect it. We never ask for bank logins.

How long does it take?

Our part takes a day or two. Each map's review usually takes a few days, and sometimes a couple of weeks. We keep checking and tell you when each one is through.
