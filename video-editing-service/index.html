The honest part

What we don't do.

We only edit footage you own or have permission to use. Your own videos, or ones you've been given the right to use. We don't re-upload other people's videos, movies or TV.
We don't add copyrighted songs. Popular songs get videos muted or claimed on YouTube and TikTok. We use music you have a license for or royalty-free music.
We don't film. There's no camera crew. You shoot it on your phone or camera and send it to us.
No fakes. No face or voice swaps of real people, no fake views, likes or followers. We don't post for you; you post the finished video.

Questions

Good questions.

Can I pay someone to edit my videos?

Yes. Send us your clips and tell us where the video is going. A real person edits it and sends you a draft to check, and you get the finished file. We send a fixed quote within one business day, before we start.

How much does it cost to pay someone to edit a video?

With us, a fixed quote within one business day, before we start, based on how much footage there is and what it needs. A short clip with captions takes less time than a long video with music, titles and sound cleanup. The quote is the price, and nothing is charged until you say yes.

Can I hire someone to edit my YouTube videos?

Yes. We cut long videos tight, clean up the sound, add captions, titles and chapters, and can make a thumbnail. You upload it to your own channel.

Can you edit TikTok videos and Reels?

Yes. We make vertical videos with big captions, from your phone clips or cut down from a longer video, sized for TikTok, Instagram Reels, Facebook and YouTube Shorts.

Can you edit a wedding or family video?

Yes. Send the clips from everyone's phones and cameras, and we put them together into one video with music you're allowed to use. We don't film the event.

How do I send you my videos?

Share a Google Drive, Dropbox or iCloud folder link with the original files. Texting a video shrinks it, so a shared folder gives a better result.

Can you add my favorite song?

Only if you own a license for it. Popular songs usually get a video muted or claimed on YouTube and TikTok, so we use royalty-free music or music you've bought the rights to.
