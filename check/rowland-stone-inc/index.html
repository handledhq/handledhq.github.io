What's tripping it up

In plain words, with how to fix each one yourself. This is yours to keep.

Pictures have no description 3 spots

Why it matters: Screen readers used by blind customers just say “image”, so they miss your work photos.

Fix it yourself: In your site editor, open each image's settings and fill in the alt text with a few words saying what the photo shows, like “new desert landscaping with river rock in Washington, UT”.

Zooming is turned off on phones 1 spot

Why it matters: People who need to pinch-zoom to read can't.

Fix it yourself: Remove “user-scalable=no” / maximum-scale from the viewport setting, or ask your web person to.

Some text is too light against its background 1 spot

Why it matters: People with weaker eyesight, or anyone on a phone in the sun, can't read it.

Fix it yourself: Darken the text color (or lighten the background) until a free contrast checker such as WebAIM's says it passes.

Headings skip levels 1 spot

Why it matters: Screen-reader users jump around a page by its headings; skipped levels make it confusing.

Fix it yourself: Use one main heading (H1), then H2s for sections and H3s inside them, in order.

Honest note: an automated test only catches part of the problems, and a score isn't a legal opinion. A person checking with a keyboard and a screen reader finds the rest.
